# N=42 dihedral symmetry reduction for the physical even-minor moment problem

Date: 2026-11-28. Run 76. Related: Issue #7 and Run 75.

## Setup

Let
\[
A(c)_{j,j+1}=2c_j,\qquad A(c)_{j+1,j}=-2c_j
\]
with cyclic indices modulo \(42\), and
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,\ldots,1,-1),\qquad
Q(c)=e^{A(c)}D.
\]
For every real coupling vector, \(Q(c)\) is a symmetric involution. The feature map of interest is
\[
v_{\rm even}(c)=\bigl(\det Q(c)_{I,I}\bigr)_{|I|\ {\rm even}}.
\]
The target \(\tau_I=(-1)^{|I|/2}\binom{21}{|I|/2}/\binom{42}{|I|}\) depends only on \(|I|\), so it is invariant under every site permutation.

## Theorem 1 — dihedral equivariance, including strictly positive couplings

Let \(r_s(i)=i+s\pmod {42}\) be a rotation and \(\rho(i)=-i\pmod {42}\) a reflection. Define transformed coupling vectors
\[
(c^{(s)})_j=c_{j-s},\qquad
(c^{(\rho)})_j=c_{-j-1}.
\]
Both transformations are permutations of the 42 coupling entries, so they preserve the strictly-positive domain as well as \(\mathbb R^{42}\).

Let \(P_g\) be the permutation matrix of a site permutation \(g\). Directly from the oriented cycle-generator definition,
\[
P_{r_s}A(c)P_{r_s}^{T}=A(c^{(s)}),
\qquad
P_{r_s}DP_{r_s}^{T}=(-1)^sD.
\]
Consequently
\[
P_{r_s}Q(c)P_{r_s}^{T}=(-1)^sQ(c^{(s)}).
\]
For an even set \(I\), multiplication of the principal block by \(-1\) changes its determinant by \((-1)^{|I|}=1\). Thus rotation permutes the even-minor coordinates exactly as it permutes subsets.

For reflection, orientation of every cycle edge is reversed:
\[
P_\rho A(c)P_\rho^{T}=-A(c^{(\rho)}),
\qquad
P_\rho DP_\rho^{T}=D.
\]
Since \(D A(c^{(\rho)})D=-A(c^{(\rho)})\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
P_\rho Q(c)P_\rho^{T}
&=e^{-A(c^{(\rho)})}D\\
&=D e^{A(c^{(\rho)})}\\
&=D Q(c^{(\rho)})D.
\end{aligned}
\]
For every principal block,
\[
\det(DQ D)_{I,I}
=\det(D_{I,I})^2\det Q_{I,I}
=\det Q_{I,I}.
\]
Therefore reflection also permutes the even-minor coordinates within the same physical feature family. This remains true when every coupling is required to be strictly positive; no sign reversal of the coupling domain is needed.

Rotations and reflections generate \(D_{42}\), of order \(84\). Hence the complete even-principal-minor feature family is \(D_{42}\)-equivariant.

## Corollary — without loss of generality, a feasible measure can be dihedrally symmetrized

Suppose a probability measure \(\mu\) on the allowed cycle couplings realizes every target even-minor moment. Average its pushforwards over the 84 dihedral transformations:
\[
\bar\mu=\frac1{84}\sum_{g\in D_{42}}g_\#\mu.
\]
Theorem 1 shows that every transformed atom remains physically allowed and that its feature vector is the corresponding coordinate permutation. Since \(\tau_I\) depends only on \(|I|\), it is fixed by this action. Therefore
\[
\int v_{\rm even}(c)\,d\bar\mu(c)=\tau
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\int v_{\rm even}(c)\,d\mu(c)=\tau.
\]
Thus any solution, if one exists, has a \(D_{42}\)-invariant representative. The same averaging preserves strict positivity of all couplings.

This is a symmetry reduction of the search space, not a feasibility or infeasibility result.

## Exact orbit counts for pairs and quartets

A standard-library bit-mask enumeration of the action generated by \(i\mapsto i+1\) and \(i\mapsto-i\) gives:

- Pair subsets: 21 dihedral orbits, with size histogram \(1\times21\) and \(20\times42\), covering all \(\binom{42}{2}=861\) pairs.
- Quartic subsets: 1,440 dihedral orbits, with size histogram \(10\times21\), \(200\times42\), and \(1,230\times84\), covering all \(\binom{42}{4}=111,930\) quartets.
- The 2,670 cyclic quartic orbits from Run 75 split into 210 reflection-stable cyclic orbits and 1,230 pairs of reflection-related cyclic orbits.

For a normalized ensemble, the Run 75 global quartic sum rule makes one weighted all-ones orbit equation redundant. Therefore a dihedrally invariant complete quartic search needs at most 1,439 independent quartic orbit coordinates, rather than the 2,669 cyclic-orbit coordinates after the same aggregate reduction. This removes 1,230 quartic rows (about 46.1%).

The count is a statement about orbit coordinates and the known aggregate identity. It does not assert that the remaining physical feature matrix has full row rank; further dependencies may exist.

## Normalization and implementation caveat

The pair identity remains
\[
2\sum_{d=1}^{20}p_d+p_{21}=-1.
\]
Enforcing all 21 pair-distance target equations therefore forces the mixture weights to sum to one. If an LP omits one pair row, it must include an explicit normalization row. Once normalized, one quartic dihedral-orbit equation can be omitted using the global quartic sum rule.

A seed-orbit column for a future search should be the average over its \(D_{42}\) coupling orbit. Stabilizers can make fewer than 84 distinct atoms; duplicates may be combined without changing the measure.

## Scope and novelty boundary

Group averaging and orbit-stabilizer counting are standard. The EUI-specific result is the exact action of \(D_{42}\) on the gauged physical feature map \(Q(c)=e^{A(c)}D\), including the diagonal-gauge identity that keeps reflection inside the strictly-positive coupling domain, and the resulting exact reduction of the N=42 quartic search. No complete moment ensemble, global separator, Choi-distance bound, or diamond-distance bound is established here.
