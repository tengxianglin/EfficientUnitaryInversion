L5: # Math.skill
L10: ## Skill Purpose
L12: Enable AI assistants to handle mathematical tasks across all difficulty levels — from basic arithmetic to research-level problems — with rigorous, step-by-step reasoning, systematic verification, and transparent uncertainty handling.
L13: This skill enforces a disciplined mathematical workflow: every problem is parsed, modeled, solved with justifications, verified through multiple independent checks, and only then delivered as a final answer. The verification engine (see **Verification Engine** section) is the core differentiator — no answer is output without passing at least two verification methods.
L31: - **Linear Algebra**: Matrices, determinants, vector spaces, linear transformations, eigenvalues/eigenvectors, diagonalization, inner product spaces, quadratic forms

L159: ### Step 5: Verification
L161: Apply at least two verification methods from the Verification Engine. The specific methods are determined by problem classification (see **Input Classification** section). Record the verification steps and their outcomes.
L177: ## Verification Engine
L179: Verification is the core of Math.skill. Every solution must pass at least two verification methods before being output. Never output an unverified solution.
L182: ### Method A: Back-Substitution
L184: Substitute the obtained solution back into the original equation(s) or conditions. Verify that all equalities hold and all inequality constraints are satisfied.

L212: Choose representative numerical values (special values: 0, 1, -1, fractions, parameter extremes) and verify both the intermediate steps and the final answer numerically.
L225: ### Method H: Independent Method Cross-Validation
L227: Solve the same problem using a completely different method. The two solutions must agree. If they disagree, at least one is wrong — re-examine both.

L249: ## Higher Mathematics Modules
L272: ### Linear Algebra
L274: - Check matrix dimension compatibility before every multiplication
L275: - Verify eigenvalues/eigenvectors by computing $A\mathbf{v} - \lambda\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$
L276: - For diagonalization, check that eigenvectors are linearly independent and that the diagonalization $A = PDP^{-1}$ reconstructs $A$
L277: - For systems $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$, state whether the solution is unique, infinite, or nonexistent based on rank analysis
L278: - Verify orthogonality claims by computing inner products
